# P1's third rating — the contrast survives a repaired sheet, and the broken one would have tripled it

Analysis registered in `plan/P1-ANALYSIS-REGISTRATION.md` before it was run, with the ratings
already collected; that registration says so, and says what this cannot deliver. Record:
`records/rate3/analysis.json`. Rater `L3` = `gpt-6-astra`.

## What P1 asked for, and what this is not

The plan asks P1 for **a human from outside the project**, because the existing +22.4-point
contrast was rated by a model and by the author, and therefore supports the *direction* of C4
rather than its *independence*. `L3` is a third model. **This does not deliver the independence
P1 exists to provide, and the outside human remains owed.** What it does deliver is a rating by
someone who is not the author, on a sheet whose control arm was unanswerable until today.

## Table 1 — undetermined specifications, missed against caught

| reading | missed | caught | difference | problem-cluster 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **primary** — `cannot-tell` in the denominator | 43/68 = 63.2% | 23/53 = 43.4% | **+19.8** | [+1.5, +38.2] |
| secondary — `cannot-tell` excluded | 43/61 = 70.5% | 23/48 = 47.9% | +22.6 | [+2.1, +42.6] |
| six-category comparator (**a different instrument**) | 46/68 = 67.6% | 24/53 = 45.3% | +22.4 | — |

Both registered readings put the difference in the same direction as the six-category figure,
and both intervals exclude zero. **The primary interval's lower end is +1.5 points.** A contrast
whose interval reaches that close to zero is consistent with a difference of almost nothing, and
nothing here licenses quoting +19.8 as though the interval were tight.

The six-category row is printed for orientation only. `ambiguous-oracle` chosen among six
options and `undetermined` chosen among three are not the same judgement, the rates are not
interchangeable, and neither row replicates the other. The agreement of their directions is
worth something; the closeness of +22.4 to +22.6 is worth nothing and is not evidence.

## Table 2 — what the broken sheet would have said

`L3` rated the unanswerable version of this sheet earlier the same day, before it was rebuilt on
mechanically recovered witnesses. That sheet showed 42 of its 53 caught items no failing input
class at all.

| sheet | missed | caught | difference | problem-cluster 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| rebuilt (primary above) | 43/68 = 63.2% | 23/53 = 43.4% | +19.8 | [+1.5, +38.2] |
| broken | 42/68 = 61.8% | 4/53 = **7.5%** | **+54.2** | [+38.8, +69.8] |

**The broken sheet would have reported a contrast 2.7 times larger, in the direction that
flatters C4, with an interval nowhere near zero.** The mechanism is entirely in the caught arm:
42 of its 53 items were rated `cannot-tell` there against 5 here, because the rater was shown
nothing to judge. 67 of the 121 labels changed between the two sheets.

This is **evidence about sheets, not about specifications.** It says nothing about what any
specification determines. What it says is that a control arm which cannot be answered does not
produce noise — it produces a large, confident, wrong number pointing the way the hypothesis
points. The defect was found and repaired before any figure was quoted, so no published number
came from it. That it was one analysis away is the part worth recording.

## Limits

* `L3` is a model. P1's independence claim is not delivered here, and no sentence in this report
  should be read as delivering it.
* The instrument changed between the six-category rating and this one. Direction is comparable;
  magnitudes are not.
* The primary interval nearly touches zero at its lower end.
* The author rated 57 of these instances previously as `L1`, so for that subset the programme
  holds a re-test, not an independent review. This report's contrast is `L3` alone and does not
  pool raters.
* `cannot-tell` remains on 12 of 121 items on the rebuilt sheet. Those items are in the primary's
  denominator; the secondary drops them and moves the difference by +2.7 points.
